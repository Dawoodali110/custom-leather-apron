From hide to hook

How Every Apron Is Made

Six careful steps, done by hand — because the details you never notice are the ones that decide how long an apron lasts.

01

Selecting the hide

Every apron starts with full-grain leather — the top layer of the hide, where the fibres are tightest and strongest. We check each piece for even thickness and a clean, natural grain before it goes anywhere near a blade.

02

Cutting the pattern

Bib, pockets, straps and corner guards are cut by hand from patterns shaped around each trade — so a barber's clipper sleeve and a chef's knife pocket sit exactly where the hand reaches for them.

03

Stitching with waxed thread

Seams are double-stitched with waxed thread, which grips the leather and shrugs off moisture. Stress points — pocket mouths and strap joins — get extra rows so they never pull loose.

04

Setting the brass

Solid brass buckles, rings and rivets are set by hand. Brass won't rust in a steamy kitchen or snap in a cold workshop, and it ages to a warm patina that matches the leather.

05

Reinforcing the corners

The bottom corners are where every apron fails first. We add a second layer of leather and rivet it in place, so the edges keep their shape for years of daily wear.

06

Engraving & final check

Finally, your logo, name or initials are engraved on the chest patch. We send a proof before engraving, then check every stitch, strap and rivet before your apron ships.

Leather care guide

How to Care for Your Leather Apron

Leather is low-maintenance, but a little care goes a long way. Follow these simple habits and your apron will stay supple and handsome for years.

  • After every use. Wipe the apron with a damp cloth and hang it up to air-dry, away from ovens, radiators and direct sun.
  • Every few months. Rub in a small amount of leather conditioner with a soft cloth to keep the leather supple and help it resist stains.
  • If it gets soaked. Blot off excess water, let it dry slowly at room temperature, then condition it once it's dry. Never use a hairdryer.
  • What to avoid. Don't machine-wash, tumble-dry or soak leather, and keep solvents and bleach away from it.
